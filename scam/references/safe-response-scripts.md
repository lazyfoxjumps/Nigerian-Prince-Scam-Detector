# Safe Response Scripts and Victim Recovery Guidance

Two purposes: (1) ready-to-paste exit lines for users who want to disengage, (2) victim recovery guidance when the user has already lost money or shared sensitive information.

All scripts in this file are in **neutral voice**, not Prince voice, so the user can copy and paste them as-is.

---

## Exit lines (no reply needed for most scams)

For confirmed scams, the safest action is no reply at all and a block. But if the user wants closure or context, these are clean exits:

### Generic, formal

> "I do not wish to continue this conversation. Please do not contact me again."

### For dating app or romance contact

> "I am ending contact. Please do not message me on any platform."

(Then block on every shared channel.)

### For "official" agency impersonator (phone or email)

> "I will contact the agency directly through their official channel. I will not respond to this number or address."

(Then hang up, do not call back any number they gave you.)

### For job recruiter scam

> "I will only proceed with the hiring process through the company's verified careers page and a verified company email address."

### For marketplace overpayment / Zelle Business pressure

> "I do not upgrade or pay fees for receiving payments. Please cancel the transaction and find another buyer."

### For tech support pop-up call

Hang up. Do not engage. If you must, one line:

> "I will not give remote access or payment. I am ending this call."

## Victim recovery guidance (in order of priority)

If the user has sent money, shared credentials, given remote access, or sent ID documents, work the list top-down.

### Within minutes / hours

1. **Stop sending anything more.** Do not "try one more payment" to "unlock" anything.
2. **Cut contact** on every channel. Do not announce that you know it is a scam (this can trigger retaliation; on rare occasions, doxxing).
3. **If money was sent via bank transfer or card**: call your bank's fraud line immediately. Use the number on the back of your card or the bank's official website, not any number the scammer gave you. Ask for a recall and to dispute the charge.
4. **If gift cards were sent**: contact the card issuer immediately. Some issuers can freeze balances if reported within hours. Save the card numbers and receipts.
5. **If crypto was sent**: report to the exchange you sent from and ask them to flag the destination address. Some exchanges cooperate with law enforcement to freeze funds at the next centralized hop. Note the transaction hash.
6. **If remote access was given**: disconnect from the internet, reboot in safe mode, run antivirus, change passwords from a different device. Assume saved passwords in browsers are compromised.
7. **If a password or OTP was shared**: change that password immediately from a different device, enable hardware-based 2FA where possible, log out all sessions, check for unauthorized account changes.
8. **If ID / selfie was sent**: place a fraud alert on your credit (US: Equifax, Experian, TransUnion; check local equivalents). Consider a credit freeze. Monitor for new accounts.

### Within 24 to 72 hours

1. **Report to the local cybercrime agency** (see `regional-scam-index.md`).
2. **File with local police** if your country requires it for bank dispute claims.
3. **Save everything**: screenshots, full message threads, transaction records, phone numbers, email headers. Do not delete.
4. **Notify your bank in writing** in addition to the call, so there is a record.
5. **If romance / pig butchering**: also report to the dating app or platform where contact began. They keep internal scammer lists.

### The recovery-scam warning (mandatory in victim mode)

Within hours to days of any large loss, the user is highly likely to be contacted by a "recovery agent", "crypto recovery firm", "FBI specialist", or "lawyer" claiming they can recover the lost funds for an upfront fee. **These are almost always second scams run by the same crews or by other groups who buy victim lists.**

Tell the user, in plain language:

> "Anyone who reaches out to you offering to recover your money for an upfront fee is a second scammer. Real law enforcement does not charge fees. Real lawyers do not cold-call victims. Real recovery firms are paid on contingency, vetted, and you find them, not the other way around. Do not pay anyone to recover your money."

This warning is non-negotiable in victim-mode reports.

### Emotional support note

The user may be ashamed. Many people, including sophisticated ones, fall for these. Lead with: "You are not stupid. These people do this for a living and they are good at it." Then move to action steps.

Do not lecture. Do not "I told you so". Do not theorize about how they should have seen it. Just give them the next three things to do.

## What NOT to include in any safe response

Even in the cleanest exit line, never include:

- Full legal name
- Address or city beyond what they already have
- Phone number not already known to the scammer
- Email aliases / secondary emails
- Any "test" payment or "small" payment
- Any apology that justifies further engagement
- Any clever line designed to "burn them" or waste their time

Brevity protects the user. The scammer wants engagement. Deny it.
