# Scam Playbooks

Each playbook lists: typical opener, stages, telltale phrases, the ask, and what "next message" looks like. Use this to identify which playbook a message fits and which stage the target is at.

---

## 1. 419 / Advance-Fee Fraud

Classic Nigerian Prince. Inheritance, lottery, abandoned funds, dying widow, oil contract.

**Stages**:
1. **Hook**: large sum of money exists, you are uniquely positioned to receive a share.
2. **Trust build**: documents, letterheads, fake government officials, sob stories.
3. **First fee**: "small" processing, courier, lawyer, or tax fee. Often 50 to 500 USD.
4. **Escalation**: complications arise, more fees needed, urgency manufactured.
5. **Endgame**: drains the target until they stop paying or run out.

**Telltale phrases**: "100% risk free", "next of kin", "blocked funds", "Central Bank of [country]", "Dr. / Barrister / Engr. [Name]", "strictly confidential", "God-fearing", "I found your contact through a directory".

**The ask**: upfront payment for "release" of a larger sum.

**Next message**: another fee, urgent, with a fabricated complication.

## 2. Romance Scam / Pig Butchering

Long-game emotional manipulation. Pig butchering specifically funnels the target into a fake crypto investment platform.

**Stages**:
1. **Contact**: dating app, social DM, "wrong number" text that turns friendly.
2. **Charm**: attractive photo (often stolen), shared values, daily messages, fast intimacy.
3. **Isolation**: discourages friends and family from "interfering". Builds dependence.
4. **Setup**: mentions crypto / forex / stock success. "My uncle taught me." Offers to teach.
5. **The platform**: directs target to a fake trading site showing fake gains.
6. **Drain**: target deposits more, sees fake profits, cannot withdraw without "tax" or "unlock fee".
7. **Endgame**: site goes dark, target ghosted.

**Telltale phrases**: "I rarely connect with people like this", "my uncle / aunt is a trader", "let me show you", "small investment", "you can withdraw anytime", "I would never lie to you", profile photos that look professional or model-tier, refuses video calls or always has "bad signal".

**The ask**: deposit into a crypto platform or wallet address. Later: "tax" or "unlock" payment.

**Next message**: a screenshot of "your" growing balance, an invitation to "increase your position".

## 3. Tech Support Scam

Microsoft, Apple, "your computer is infected", "your account is compromised".

**Stages**:
1. **Alert**: pop-up, email, or unsolicited call claims infection or breach.
2. **Hook number**: target calls a "support" number.
3. **Remote access**: pushed to install AnyDesk, TeamViewer, or similar.
4. **Fake fix**: scammer runs harmless commands (netstat, tree) and calls them "evidence of intrusion".
5. **Payment**: charges hundreds for "removal" via gift cards, wire, or crypto. Often steals bank credentials during the session.

**Telltale phrases**: "Microsoft Security", "Apple Support", "your IP has been compromised", "do not turn off your computer", "you must act now".

**The ask**: remote access + payment in gift cards or wire.

**Next message**: follow-up call to "renew" the protection, or to "refund" you (a refund scam variant).

## 4. Fake Authority (IRS / Tax / Police / Immigration)

Threats of arrest, deportation, or seizure. Often robocalls or texts.

**Stages**:
1. **Threat**: "warrant for your arrest", "tax debt", "visa cancelled", "court summons".
2. **Pressure**: short window, no time to verify, "call this number now".
3. **Payment**: settle in gift cards (Apple, Google Play, Target), wire, or crypto. Real agencies never do this.

**Telltale phrases**: "final notice", "warrant", "do not hang up", "this is a recorded line", official-sounding case numbers, threats of arrest within hours.

**The ask**: immediate payment via untraceable methods.

**Next message**: escalation ("the officers are on their way"), or a fake "officer" calls.

## 5. Job / Recruiter Scam

Remote job, easy money, often with a "starter kit" or "check deposit" twist.

**Stages**:
1. **Outreach**: unsolicited offer via LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Telegram, Indeed.
2. **Interview**: text-only or chat-only "interview". No video. Quick hire.
3. **Onboarding**: asked to buy equipment ("we will reimburse"), deposit a check then send most of it elsewhere ("payroll software"), or share ID + bank details.
4. **Loss**: equipment payment is gone, deposited check bounces in a week, identity is stolen.

**Telltale phrases**: "easy work from home", "no experience needed", "Telegram interview", "we will send a check for equipment", "personal shopper", "mystery shopper", "package forwarder".

**The ask**: bank details, ID documents, or money sent onward from a fake check.

**Next message**: the "check" arrives, urgency to deposit and forward funds.

## 6. Marketplace Overpayment / Payment App

Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist, OfferUp. Zelle, Venmo, Cash App.

**Stages**:
1. **Buyer / seller contact**: too easy, no negotiation, eager.
2. **Off-platform**: pushes to Zelle, Venmo, Cash App, or a shipping site.
3. **Overpayment**: "accidentally" sends too much, asks for refund of the difference.
4. **Reversal**: original payment was fraudulent or reversed. Target loses the "refund".

**Telltale phrases**: "I'll send my shipper", "Zelle Business upgrade needed", "I overpaid, please refund", "my husband will pick it up".

**The ask**: refund the difference, or pay a "Zelle Business" fee.

**Next message**: screenshot of fake email "from Zelle" demanding the fee.

## 7. Inheritance / Lottery / Parcel

You won, you inherited, a package is held at customs.

**Stages**:
1. **Notification**: you won a lottery you never entered, a relative you never knew died, a parcel awaits.
2. **Fee**: tax, customs, courier, legal release.
3. **Drain**: same as 419.

**Telltale phrases**: "congratulations", "selected at random", "customs holding fee", "DHL / FedEx" with a non-FedEx tracking site.

**The ask**: release fee.

**Next message**: another release fee, "almost there".

---

## 8. AI Voice-Clone "Grandparent" Scam

Phone call, often from "grandchild" or "child" in distress. Voice may be cloned from social media.

**Stages**:
1. **Panic call**: "Grandma, I'm in jail, I had an accident, don't tell mom and dad".
2. **Handoff**: "lawyer" or "officer" takes the phone.
3. **Payment**: bail, hospital, lawyer fee. Cash courier, gift cards, wire, crypto.

**Telltale phrases**: garbled voice, "don't tell my parents", insistence on secrecy, courier arriving at the house.

**The ask**: immediate cash, no time to verify.

**Next message**: follow-up "complication" requiring more cash.

---

## 9. Recovery Scam

Targets people who already lost money. Poses as a recovery agent, lawyer, FBI specialist, blockchain forensics firm.

**Stages**:
1. **Cold approach**: reaches out to known victim (sometimes within days of original loss, victim lists are sold between criminal groups).
2. **Credibility**: fake credentials, fake case numbers, fake testimonials.
3. **Retainer**: asks for upfront fee to begin recovery.
4. **Drain**: more fees for "tax", "court", "unlock".

**Telltale phrases**: "we specialize in crypto recovery", "we have a 90% success rate", "we are working with [agency]", "small retainer to begin".

**The ask**: upfront recovery fee.

**Why this matters**: when the user is in victim mode, the recovery-scam warning is the single most important safety message. Lists of victims circulate. The second hit is often worse than the first.

---

## Stage labeling

When labeling target stage in the report, use:

- **Stage 0**: unsolicited contact, no engagement yet.
- **Stage 1**: target replied, scammer building trust.
- **Stage 2**: first ask landed (fee, info, credentials), not yet paid.
- **Stage 3**: target paid or sent info once.
- **Stage 4**: ongoing extraction.
- **Stage 5**: ghosted or recovery-scam window.
