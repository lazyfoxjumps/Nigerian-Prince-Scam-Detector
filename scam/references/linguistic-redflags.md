# Linguistic Red Flags

Score the message across five axes. Each axis runs 0 (absent) to 3 (heavy). Total over 8 is strong signal; over 12 is near certainty when combined with one other check.

## 1. Urgency (0 to 3)

- "Act now", "within 24 hours", "final notice", "your account will be closed".
- Countdown timers.
- Threats of arrest, account closure, deportation, or loss within a tight window.
- "Do not hang up", "do not turn off your computer", "do not tell anyone".

**Why it works**: urgency suppresses verification. Real institutions tolerate verification calls; scammers cannot.

## 2. Secrecy (0 to 3)

- "Do not tell your family", "this is confidential", "between us only", "your bank will not understand".
- Discourages talking to a partner, parent, friend, or professional.
- Insists on a private channel (Telegram, WhatsApp, off-platform).

**Why it works**: isolation removes the outside reality check.

## 3. Authority (0 to 3)

- Claims of official titles: "Barrister", "Dr.", "Engr.", "Officer", "Agent", "Director".
- Letterheads, case numbers, badge numbers, fake government stamps.
- Spoofed sender names ("Apple Support", "Microsoft Security", "IRS Tax Division").
- Threats backed by claimed legal power.

**Why it works**: people defer to perceived authority, especially under stress.

## 4. Reward (0 to 3)

- Large sum offered for small action: "claim your $5,000,000", "I will share 30% with you".
- Lottery wins, inheritance, abandoned funds, surprise refunds.
- Job offers with unrealistic pay for unclear work.
- Investment "guarantees" (any guaranteed return is a red flag).

**Why it works**: dopamine hit on the reward overrides skepticism about the path to it.

## 5. Grammar fingerprints and AI tells (0 to 3)

Classic human-scammer grammar:
- Title Case In The Middle Of Sentences.
- Excessive politeness ("kindly do the needful", "I humbly request").
- Mixed UK / US spelling in one paragraph.
- Strange capitalization of GOD, COMPANY, BANK.
- Religious framing ("God-fearing", "in the name of the Almighty").
- Awkward titles: "Barrister Mr." "Dr. Mrs. Engr.".

Newer AI tells:
- Hyper-polished but generic prose.
- Em-dashes everywhere (current LLMs love them).
- Tri-colon rhythms ("I will help you. I will guide you. I will protect you.").
- Vague specifics ("a leading institution", "a major bank").
- Boilerplate empathy ("I understand this can be a difficult time").
- Lists of three. Always lists of three.
- Phrases like "It's not just X, it's Y" or "It's worth noting that".

Mixed AI + human tells (most common in 2026):
- A polished opener followed by a clumsy ask.
- Correct grammar paragraph 1, broken English paragraph 3.
- AI-generated romance opener, hand-typed crypto-platform instructions.

## Bonus signals (count as +1 each)

- Payment in gift cards (Apple, Google Play, Target, Steam).
- Crypto-only payment, especially USDT on Tron.
- Requests for screenshots of ID, selfie holding ID, or bank statements.
- Refusal of video call after weeks of contact.
- Profile photo that looks like a stock image or model.
- Phone number country code does not match claimed location.
- Reply-to address differs from From address.
- Domain registered in the last 30 days.

## Scoring guide

- **0 to 4**: low signal, may be legitimate.
- **5 to 7**: suspicious, deserves verification.
- **8 to 11**: probably a scam, do not engage casually.
- **12+**: scam, near certainty.

Always combine linguistic score with at least one entity check or header check before publishing a high-confidence verdict.
